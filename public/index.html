      How it works
      1. Read. Files are read in your browser (PDF with pdf.js, Word with mammoth). Project Gutenberg boilerplate is removed.
      2. Chunk. Text is split at paragraph and sentence boundaries into ~1,000-character chunks with ~200 characters of overlap. Each chunk gets an ID like D2-C0153: document 2, chunk 153.
      3. Embed and store. Each chunk is turned into a vector with all-MiniLM-L6-v2, on your graphics chip and processor at once. The store stays in your browser and can be downloaded.
      4. Search. Each question and two rephrasings are matched by meaning and by keywords, and the results are combined and de-duplicated into the best 8 chunks.
      5. Gate. If nothing found is close enough, the answer is “not in the sources” and the LLM is never asked.
      6. Answer. Gemini sees only those 8 chunks and must give each sentence the IDs it came from, or say “not in the sources.”
      7. Check. Code checks every sentence: the IDs must exist and must have been retrieved, and every name and number must appear in the cited chunk. Sentences that fail are dropped.
      8. Story. Gemini weaves the checked answers into a story, skipping what the sources don’t cover. The story is checked the same way, then audited by a second LLM pass that sees only the cited chunks.

      Built for MCON 368. Pages and embeddings stay in your browser; only the 8 chunks for each question are sent to Gemini.
